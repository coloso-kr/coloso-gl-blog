## 💡 Pastrychef Sejung Kim's Essential Scone Wisdom

### 1. Mastering Dough Consistency: Beyond Basic Mixing
Pastrychef Sejung Kim emphasizes that the dough is the foundation. For a less watery dough that's easier to handle and yields a texture closer to traditional scones, the method of incorporating **melted butter with cold cream** is key. While grinding butter into flour is a classic approach, this alternative method creates small butter clumps within the cream, mimicking the effect without the extra dishes. This technique ensures a more uniform distribution, preventing an overly bread-like texture. Sejung's insight: "It won't be exactly the same as ground butter, but it'd be close enough for an easier bake."

### 2. The Art of Jam Stabilization: The 3-Minute Bake
A common issue is jam sliding off or becoming overly hard and caramelized during baking. Pastrychef Sejung Kim’s pro move is to **bake the scones for an additional 3 minutes *after* applying the jam**. This crucial step stabilizes the jam, preventing it from sliding off and maintaining its fresh fruit flavor. Sejung notes, "If we bake them in this state [without the extra bake], the jam will bake for 20 minutes and become very hard and crumbly." This technique ensures the jam stays put, looks appealing, and retains its vibrant taste.

### 3. Flavor Infusion: Balancing Lemon and Pistachio
Achieving complex flavors like lemon pistachio requires careful ingredient integration. For the lemon curd, Pastrychef Sejung Kim highlights the importance of **avoiding direct contact between egg yolk and lemon juice** before blending, as the acidity can prematurely harden the yolk. Using a **heat-resistant silicon whisk** is also recommended to prevent metallic flavors and ensure thorough mixing. For pistachio scones, Sejung prefers to **add pistachio paste and liquid ingredients to the nuts before grinding**, allowing the oil to release naturally, preserving the nut’s distinct aroma without roasting.

## 🛠️ Key Skills & Details That Define Professional Quality
![]()

The difference between a good scone and a great one often lies in the details Pastrychef Sejung Kim meticulously addresses. For instance, when making jam, using a **ratio of 1:0.7 fruit to sugar** provides a balanced sweetness, while adding sugar incrementally helps control caramelization and preserve fruit freshness. When testing jam consistency, a quick dip in **ice water** allows for an accurate assessment of the final thickness. For lemon curd, Sejung’s tip to **wrap the surface tightly with plastic wrap before chilling** prevents moisture loss and contamination. These precise techniques, learned through years of experience at her renowned "Spread" bakeshop, are what elevate home baking to a professional standard.

## 💬 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Pastrychef Sejung Kim, how can I prevent my scones from smelling like flour?**

A. Pastrychef Sejung Kim advises that a floury smell often indicates underbaking. This can happen if the jam is added too early, preventing moisture from escaping the center. Applying jam for the final 3 minutes of baking, as demonstrated, helps ensure the scone is cooked through while keeping the jam fresh.

**Q. Pastrychef Sejung Kim, what's the best way to get a consistent scone size?**

A. Pastrychef Sejung Kim stresses the importance of dividing the dough evenly. For 10 pieces, aim for each piece to be around 100g to 120g. While exact precision isn't always necessary, significant weight differences can lead to uneven baking when placed in the oven together.

**Q. Pastrychef Sejung Kim, can I use store-bought jam in my scones?**

A. While Pastrychef Sejung Kim demonstrates how to make homemade jam for optimal flavor control, store-bought jam can be used. However, applying it for a short, final baking period (around 3 minutes) is still crucial to stabilize it on the scone and prevent it from over-baking or sliding off.
