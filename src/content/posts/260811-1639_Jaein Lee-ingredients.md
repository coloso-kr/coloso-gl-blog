## 💡 Jaein Lee's Core Principles for Elevated Baking

### 1. The Art of Ingredient Synergy: Beyond Basic Recipes
Pastrychef Jaein Lee emphasizes that understanding the functional properties of each ingredient is crucial. For instance, the choice between different coffee forms for madeleines isn't just about flavor; it's about moisture content and how it affects the final texture. Using freeze-dried coffee powder, combined with finely ground coffee from a professional shop, offers a balanced flavor without introducing excess moisture that could compromise the delicate crumb. This approach isn't just about following a recipe; it's about understanding *why* certain ingredients behave as they do, allowing for greater control and predictable results.

### 2. Streamlining Production with Smart Ingredient Prep
For bakery owners, efficiency is paramount. Pastrychef Jaein Lee shares a practical tip for madeleines: preparing and roasting pecans in advance ensures their crispness and optimal flavor. they advise roasting them at 170 degrees Celsius for seven minutes in a convection oven, focusing on achieving a good baked flavor while keeping the inside white for better texture. Furthermore, slightly warming milk to 40-50 degrees Celsius aids in dissolving freeze-dried coffee powder effectively, preventing lumps that can't be fixed later. This meticulous pre-treatment ensures a smoother dough mixing process and better oven spring.

### 3. Elevating Texture and Shelf-Life with Dough Aging
The stability and texture of dough are significantly improved through proper aging. Pastrychef Jaein Lee highlights that dough for products like madeleines should ideally be aged for more than one night in the refrigerator. This cooling period stabilizes the air within the dough, leading to larger oven spring and a more consistent, desirable texture. For storage in piping bags, they recommend using stout, 21-inch piping bags and securely tying them with masking tape to maintain freshness. This simple step of allowing the dough to rest and mature is a cornerstone for achieving professional-grade quality and extending the product's lifespan.

## 🛠️ Key Skills & Details That Define Professional Quality

When aiming for the kind of quality found in "JAEIN," Pastry Chef Jaein Lee’s expertise shines through in the practical application of confectionery techniques. For example, in making corn cookies, she opts for Anchor butter, explaining that while European fermented butters are excellent, Anchor butter provides a good balance of quality and cost-effectiveness for a high-plasticity dough. The creaming method is detailed not just as mixing butter and sugar, but as an intentional process of incorporating air to create a fine paste, rather than a lumpy state. This attention to the physical state of the dough, achieved by mixing at speed 1 initially and then raising to speed 2, is critical for a tender cookie.

Furthermore, the choice of salt is presented as a nuanced decision. Morton Salt is preferred over refined salt due to its thorough pretreatment, which minimizes moisture absorption over time. This accuracy in measurement is essential for consistent results, especially when combined with baking soda and baking powder for optimal oven spring and stability. Pastry Chef Jaein Lee also stresses the importance of using a specific canned baking powder from Sunin, noting its role in achieving desired oven spring without the bitterness sometimes associated with other brands. These specific ingredient choices and preparation methods are not arbitrary; they are the foundation for reproducible, high-quality baked goods that can stand up to the demands of a busy bakery.

## 💬 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. How does Pastrychef Jaein Lee manage oven temperature fluctuations with an ordinary oven?**

Pastrychef Jaein Lee emphasizes adjusting oven degrees according to the day's temperature and humidity, suggesting this flexibility is key when using standard equipment. She also details specific baking temperatures and times for recipes, such as 170°C for 7 minutes for roasting pecans, and 185°C for 12 minutes for baking madeleines, providing precise guidance for common ovens.

**Q. What is Pastrychef Jaein Lee's advice on ingredient sourcing for small bakeries?**

Pastrychef Jaein Lee advocates for using readily available ingredients and simpler processes. For instance, they use canned corn for cookies to maintain rich flavor while managing unit costs, and suggests using readily available honey instead of trimoline if needed for dough recipes. The focus is on maximizing flavor and quality with accessible ingredients, often sourced domestically, to fit Korean tastes.

**Q. How can Pastrychef Jaein Lee's techniques help expand a bakery's menu?**

Pastrychef Jaein Lee’s approach to ingredient understanding allows for flexible recipe adaptation. By mastering the core principles behind her 10 signature recipes, bakers can potentially create 30-40 variations by adjusting seasonal ingredients or toppings. they demonstrate this with corn cookies, which serve as a base for various seasonal adaptations, empowering bakers to innovate and expand their offerings efficiently.
